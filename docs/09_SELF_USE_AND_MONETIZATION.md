# 09 — Self-Use First and Monetization Plan

**Strategy:** dogfood Mini Genspark as our own personal AI workspace, then monetize the proven work output and workflows before asking people to pay for a generic AI chat interface.

## 1. Product promise

Mini Genspark starts as an owner-only personal AI workspace that helps convert one request into a useful work product:
- a report with traceable sources;
- a structured brief or document;
- a reviewed data analysis;
- a coding task plan and patch workflow;
- a reusable sequence of steps for work we repeat.

The promise is reliability, visible evidence, editable outputs, reuse and cost control. The promise is not "all Genspark features for free" and not unlimited model usage.

## 2. Dogfood before launch

For the first 14 days, use Mini Genspark to complete at least 10 real work sessions and record:
- task requested and output expected;
- whether the output was actually used;
- time saved (rough estimate versus doing the task manually);
- corrections and rework;
- source or factual errors;
- model/provider and known quota consumption;
- feature used and feature missing.

Start with five repeatable owner workflows:
1. Research brief with primary sources, independent cross-checks and dates.
2. Turn rough voice/text notes into a project brief and action checklist.
3. Convert the brief into an implementation prompt for a coding agent.
4. Review a repository/patch and produce acceptance criteria plus QA report.
5. Analyze a small CSV and generate a structured summary.

The first metrics are task completion rate, output correction time, repeat use, verified source rate and cost/usage per useful output. A UI that looks good but is rarely used is not product validation.

## 3. What customers should pay for

A generic model picker is easy to copy and competes with established providers' free tiers. Do not sell the model itself or repackage provider free quotas.

Sell one of these only after we can deliver it reliably:
- a defined research deliverable with traceable sources;
- a repeatable workflow bundle that saves a user time;
- a configured private workspace with clear setup, privacy and support;
- a team workflow with shared artifacts, audit and access control.

### Monetization sequence

**Stage A — founder use (private, no charge)**
- Use Mini Genspark as a tool to make our own work faster.
- Do not invite public users or collect their sensitive documents before access control/privacy are ready.

**Stage B — service-backed paid pilot (first revenue candidate)**
- Offer a narrowly scoped deliverable, not broad software access.
- Example offers to test: a competitor/market scan with source ledger; a reviewed research brief; a codebase/website audit with prioritized fixes; or a proposal/operating document.
- Send a sample output and transparent scope. Produce the output with Mini Genspark plus human verification.
- Accept a real purchase/payment through a chosen compliant channel. Do not interpret compliments or survey interest as payment evidence.

**Stage C — repeatable workflow pack**
- Turn the workflow that sold into a documented, versioned recipe with required inputs, outputs, source rules, QA checklist, and examples.
- Charge for a workflow pack or the result it produces; include maintenance/support only if we can deliver it.

**Stage D — BYOK workspace**
- Let advanced users connect their own provider credentials where the provider permits this use.
- Charge for workspace features (workflow management, history, project context, artifacts, private deployment or team controls), not for hidden resale of a third-party free API.
- Securely store keys only server-side; allow revocation; never log key values.

**Stage E — hosted team plan**
- Consider subscriptions only after we know active usage, per-user provider/storage costs, support burden, and repeat purchase.
- The product needs a reliable capability and cost dashboard before this is economically safe.

## 4. Initial price experiments (hypotheses only)

These are test prices, not researched market averages or validated willingness-to-pay:

| Offer | Test price | What buyer receives |
|---|---:|---|
| One-off source-backed brief | IDR 49,000–149,000 | Defined question, stated source count/coverage, dated evidence table, findings and limitations |
| Small business workflow/audit | IDR 99,000–299,000 | One scoped workflow review, prioritized improvements, examples, and action checklist |
| Setup / workflow customization | IDR 149,000–499,000 | Configuration and handover for one clearly defined workflow |
| Downloadable reusable workflow pack | IDR 29,000–99,000 | Workflow, input template, QA checklist and example artifacts |

Do not publish all offers as a large menu immediately. Choose one based on reachable customers and evidence. Start with a paid pilot and measure the hours required to fulfil it. Price must cover the real work, transaction fees, support and future tooling—not only model inference.

## 5. What should remain free?

For a first personal/solo tier, consider:
- local/demo mode;
- a small quota for own workspace tasks;
- standard artifact types (Markdown/HTML/CSV);
- access to reusable basic workflows.

Potential paid differentiators after validation:
- saved/reusable multi-step workflows;
- advanced source verification and recurring reports;
- richer artifact formats and version history;
- project knowledge collections;
- private workspace setup and export;
- team sharing, approval and audit.

A free tier must be quota-limited by design. Never promise unlimited inference under finite third-party free quotas.

## 6. Unit economics and abuse prevention

For each paid pilot or eventual subscription, record:
- revenue received and refunds;
- processing/payment fees;
- provider usage and known cost;
- storage and egress;
- human verification/support time;
- unsuccessful runs and reruns;
- gross margin before general overhead.

Do not provide free provider calls to anonymous end users via a shared founder key. That can exhaust quotas, violate provider terms, expose data or create unexpected costs. Public signup must wait until authentication, per-user quotas, abuse controls and privacy practices are tested.

## 7. Go/no-go gates

Go to a small external pilot only if:
- there are at least three workflows we use repeatedly;
- at least one workflow produces a clearly useful result with acceptable manual correction;
- provider limits and data-retention implications are understood;
- no critical secrets/auth/privacy defect remains.

Go to a paid workflow pilot only if:
- prospective users outside close friends can be reached;
- the deliverable and expected result can be described precisely;
- scope, turnaround, revision policy and refund terms are written;
- sample quality is strong enough to be sold honestly.

Go to SaaS subscriptions only after at least three independent paying customers and repeated use provide evidence that the workflow should be software rather than a one-off service. This is a conservative internal gate, not a universal industry rule.

## 8. Moat direction

Mini Genspark's defensibility should come from:
- a tested workflow library and its acceptance tests;
- a record of where research/outputs fail and how the product detects the failure;
- source/evidence normalization and quality review;
- user-owned project context and reusable artifacts;
- distribution and trust with a specific class of customer.

"Uses many AI models" is not a moat. A generic prompt, model router or copied UI alone is not a durable advantage.

## Decision

Use Mini Genspark privately first. In parallel, identify one paid output we can produce using it. Sell the result with human quality control, learn from paid work, then productize the repeated workflow. Delay subscriptions and broad public access until value and unit economics are supported by evidence.
