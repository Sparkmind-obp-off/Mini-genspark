# VestrenHQ — ICP and Customer Discovery Playbook

**Status:** validation protocol, not evidence that product-market fit exists. Review after every five interviews.

## Objective
Find one customer segment with a repeated, costly-enough job Vestren can solve reliably and profitably. Validate a real job, current workaround, urgency, frequency, buyer, and willingness to pay—not the broad claim that people need AI.

## Initial segment hypotheses

| Segment | Repeated job hypothesis | Trigger | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo founders / small business operators | Research a market, competitor, product, or business decision and produce an action brief | New offer, pricing decision, competitor change | Recent example, current workflow, decision impact, frequency |
| Independent consultants / service providers | Turn research into a client-ready brief or proposal | New prospect, client question, project kickoff | Time spent, quality bar, client deliverable, willingness to pay |
| Small product / growth teams | Compare options and synthesize sourced findings | Campaign, launch, vendor selection | Existing tools, review steps, buyer, security needs |
| Builders / technical founders | Turn a technical/business question into a verified implementation plan | Feature decision, integration, repository task | Required evidence, execution needs, cost of wrong answer |

These are competing hypotheses, not final market facts. Choose one segment only after interview evidence supports it.

## Qualification rubric
Score each prospect 0–2: 0 absent, 1 partial, 2 strong.
- Task occurred in the last 30 days.
- Task repeats at least monthly.
- Current workaround consumes meaningful time or creates risk.
- Prospect can show a sanitized prior output/workflow.
- Prospect can decide or influence purchase.
- Prospect agrees to test on a real task.
- Prospect will discuss a paid pilot after seeing value.

10–14: priority design-partner candidate. 6–9: continue discovery. 0–5: do not prioritize. This is an internal heuristic, not an industry benchmark.

## Interview protocol (20–30 minutes)
1. Walk me through the last time you had to research or decide this.
2. What triggered it, and what happened next?
3. Which sources and tools did you use? What did you trust least?
4. How long did it take to produce something someone could act on?
5. What did you have to redo or verify manually?
6. What happens if the work is late or wrong?
7. How often does this occur, and who else does it?
8. Can you show a sanitized example of the final output?
9. Would you test a workflow on the next real task? What would block that?
10. After the test: did you use the output, what changed, and what would make it worth paying for?

Do not pitch during the first half. Record the prospect’s own words, current workaround, actual frequency, consequences, and next commitment. Do not treat hypothetical purchase interest as proof.

## Evidence ledger
Record anonymized prospect ID, segment, date, recent trigger, job, workaround/time, frequency, consequence, voluntary budget/tool context, permissioned quote snippets, qualification score and rationale, next action, pilot outcome, repeat-use evidence, and payment evidence. Do not publish names, quotes, logos, or results without explicit permission. Minimize personal data.

## Design-partner protocol
Recruit 3–5 candidates with the same job. Agree on one real task, expected deliverable, permitted inputs/data sensitivity, current limitations, founder assistance, usage cap, support route, and success criteria. Observe task completion. Measure time-to-useful-output, material corrections, source failures, artifact use, and repeat within seven days.

## Decision gates
- Continue: at least three users complete and repeat the same workflow, the output is used, and quality/cost are measurable.
- Narrow: one subtask consistently creates value but the broader workflow does not.
- Reposition: another segment shows stronger repeated pain and buyer access.
- Pause: users do not repeat, outputs are not used, or cost/security prevents delivery.

Compliments, waitlist signups, generated output volume, and founder enthusiasm do not substitute for observed use or payment.

## Weekly report
Report interviews, qualified prospects, observed tasks, first-run completion, seven-day repeat, confirmed usefulness, corrections, time saved, cost per successful run, paid offers, payments/refunds, top objections, and next experiment. State sample size and dates. Never extrapolate a small pilot into an unsupported market-size claim.
