# Vestren — Pricing and Go-to-Market Plan

**Status:** hypotheses to validate, not published offers or a guarantee of unit economics.

## 1. Positioning

**Category:** AI work execution workspace.
**V1 wedge:** source-backed research briefs and editable action deliverables for solo operators and small teams.
**Positioning sentence:** “Turn a business question into a traceable brief and a clear next step.”
**Why trust us:** sources are visible, limits are explicit, outputs are editable, and the product reports what it actually completed.

Avoid generic “AI for everything” messaging. Lead with the job, example output, and time/risk saved.

## 2. First customers

Recruit 10–15 interviewees who regularly conduct market/competitor research, vendor comparison, or prepare business briefs. Seek 3–5 design partners with real recurring tasks. Do not count friends who merely say the idea sounds good as validated demand.

Interview questions:
- Tell me about the last time you did this task.
- What triggered it, and what did you use?
- How long did it take? Which part was frustrating or risky?
- What happened because the work was late or incomplete?
- Can you show a sanitized example of the final deliverable?
- How often does this happen?
- Who decides and pays for a tool?
- Would you try this on a real task this week? What would stop you?
- After a real trial, would you pay for continued use? At what price and why?

Do not lead with “Would you buy my AI app?” and do not treat compliments as purchase intent.

## 3. Launch sequence

### Stage A — private dogfood
Use the product on real tasks. Record failure cases, time to first useful output, source quality, edit rate, and whether the deliverable was used. No public claims of production readiness.

### Stage B — design-partner pilot
Invite 3–5 users individually. Agree on the task, expected deliverable, data sensitivity, support channel, and feedback session. Disclose that this is a limited pilot and list known limitations. Avoid sensitive customer data until controls are verified.

### Stage C — paid pilot
Charge only after the launch-blocking product, security, data, support, and payment gates pass. Offer a clear, limited scope and transparent usage cap. If manually assisted onboarding is needed, disclose it rather than implying full automation.

### Stage D — public self-serve
Open sign-up only after onboarding, billing, cancellation, deletion, support, and incident handling are proven. Keep a rollback and kill-switch procedure.

## 4. Pricing experiment

Do not publish final pricing before cost measurement and willingness-to-pay interviews. Test a simple structure:
- **Free trial / starter:** capped usage, no hidden charges, no promise of unlimited access.
- **Solo:** one user, recurring projects, defined monthly usage.
- **Team:** later, only after collaboration and team authorization exist.

Set numeric prices after measuring provider cost per successful workflow, support time, payment fees, taxes, and desired gross margin. Never promise a “free forever” plan until provider quotas and sustainability are verified.

## 5. Acquisition experiments

Run small, measurable experiments rather than building a large content machine first:
- Direct outreach to interviewees with a specific example deliverable.
- Before/after sample brief showing sources, uncertainties, and action plan.
- Practical posts demonstrating one real workflow, not vague AI hype.
- Referral request after a user confirms a deliverable was useful.
- One landing page with clear ICP, sample output, limitations, privacy link, pricing/CTA, and support contact.

For each experiment track: people reached, qualified conversations, product trials, completed first workflow, repeat use in 7 days, paid conversion, refund/cancellation reasons, and support hours. No fabricated testimonials, logos, customer counts, or outcome claims.

## 6. Go/no-go thresholds (initial hypotheses)

Review after the first 10 real users; these are decision aids, not industry benchmarks:
- At least 5 complete the core workflow without founder intervention.
- At least 3 return for a second real task within 7 days.
- At least 3 say the result was used in a real decision/work product.
- At least 2 show credible willingness to pay; ideally secure a paid pilot.
- No unresolved cross-tenant data exposure, fabricated citation, or unbounded spend issue.

If demand is weak, narrow the ICP/workflow before adding modes. If source quality or repeat use is weak, improve the core workflow before spending on acquisition.

## 7. Weekly operating review

Every week record:
- customer conversations and strongest verbatim pain evidence;
- activation and repeat-use counts;
- successful versus failed workflow runs;
- citation/source quality failures;
- median time to useful deliverable;
- cost per successful run and quota exhaustion;
- paid conversions, refunds, cancellations, and support burden;
- next experiment and a single owner.

The primary milestone is not “launch the website.” It is repeatable customer value with sustainable costs and a credible reason to pay.
